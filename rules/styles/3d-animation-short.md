# 3D Animation Short

Use a story-first stylized 3D animation language suited to a short H3 generation, not a full studio-production workflow.

## Visual language

- Unless the user requests otherwise, use high-end stylized feature-animation rendering: simplified geometric character design, clear silhouettes, tactile materials, warm subsurface skin or translucent edge lighting, sculpted hair/fur clumps with fine rim detail, and appealing rather than anatomically rigid proportions.
- Avoid photorealistic live action, flat 2D anime, plastic toy skin, mannequin posing, lifeless faces, and physically unmotivated material changes.
- Lock each character's silhouette, proportions, face, hairstyle, costume colors, and signature props across all shots. Lock environment layout, light direction, landmarks, and prop positions when shots share a scene.

## Story and performance

- Build a compact causal arc that fits the provided duration: readable setup, active desire or problem, escalation caused by character action, emotional turn, and visual payoff. Coincidence must not solve the problem.
- Give each shot one dominant story action. Use anticipation, line of action, squash and stretch, elastic but readable timing, eye and brow changes, cheek and mouth shapes, hand gestures, weight shifts, and reaction beats.
- Reuse an earlier emotional or visual anchor in the ending when possible.

## Camera, continuity, and audio

- Use shot sizes and camera movement to support performance rather than decorate it. Preserve screen direction, eyelines, spatial landmarks, prop state, and action handoff across cuts.
- Time dialogue and reactions so mouth movement, pauses, and physical responses fit the shot duration.
- Use environment-specific sound effects and restrained music that follows the emotional curve without burying dialogue.
- Do not put storyboard labels, arrows, panel borders, production notes, or character-card text into the generated video.
