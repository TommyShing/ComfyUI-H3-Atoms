# Brand Promo

Create a concise, product-specific promotional film from only the supplied or explicitly stated brand facts and assets.

## Truth and identity

- Preserve supplied logos, wordmarks, product UI, packaging, people, mascots, fonts, colors, product shapes, and visible claims. Never redraw, approximate, or invent an identity-bearing asset.
- Do not invent features, metrics, slogans, endorsements, legal claims, or a CTA. If a detail is unavailable, omit it or keep it generic rather than presenting fiction as fact.
- Procedural gradients, grids, glows, particles, trails, masks, and transition geometry may support the film but must not impersonate product evidence.

## Story spine

- Choose the most suitable compact spine: AI/SaaS = intent → capability → execution → useful output → proof → logo; physical product = reveal → interaction → feature macro → use → result → logo; service = context → process → evidence → outcome → promise → logo; image-led brand = authentic imagery → motif → benefit → emotional payoff → logo.
- Fit approximately 5–8 major beats into a 15-second film, scaled to the actual duration. Each beat has one visual owner, one primary action, one proof or benefit, and enough hold time for any copy.

## Motion and copy

- Let product motion, cursor paths, UI flow, object edges, light, scrolling content, or matched geometry drive transitions.
- Build 2–3 energy peaks with quieter braking moments. Preserve clean silhouettes, logo clear space, and readable copy.
- Use only necessary visible copy. Match its language to the campaign context and supplied brand material. Keep it brief, stable, and integrated into the composition.
- Avoid fake HUDs, arbitrary glass cards, decorative text walls, unverified numbers, generic “tech” effects, and identical easing in every beat.

## Audio

- Use brand-appropriate instrumental music, interface sounds, product sounds, ambience, or supplied narration. Keep dialogue and claims intelligible and synchronized.
