# Music Video with Lyric Typography

Design music, performance, camera, editing, and visible lyric typography as one synchronized audiovisual system.

## Audio and lyrics

- Treat supplied audio as the master timeline. Map shots, cuts, gestures, camera accents, and typography events to identifiable beats, breaths, lyric phrases, snares, bass hits, or drops.
- Supplied lyrics are exact and locked: do not rewrite, translate, extend, or substitute them unless explicitly requested. Visible lyric text must match the performed words exactly.
- Do not invent lyrics when none are supplied. A no-lyric request may still use abstract typography only if explicitly requested.

## Reference roles

- Keep character identity, wardrobe, proportions, and performance presence separate from scene atmosphere and from typography packaging. A typography reference controls font texture, graphic layout, and motion—not people or locations.

## Shot and edit language

- Fit the shot count to the node duration. Give every shot an audio interval, visual action, camera behavior, vocal event, one principal typography event, and a transition cue.
- Use beat-aligned hard cuts, jump cuts, action matches, same-direction motion, occlusion cuts, or controlled glitch/flash cuts when the requested genre supports them. Do not default to fades or soft dissolves for high-energy styles.
- Preserve global character, wardrobe, aspect ratio, grain, grade, light direction, and typography rules across shots.
- Keep typography spatial: foreground, midground, background, surface projection, or partial occlusion. Never cover eyes or the main facial expression; avoid the mouth during critical lip sync.
- Keep vocal mouth shapes, jaw motion, breath, nods, hands, and facial accents synchronized to phrasing.

## Genre adaptation

- Adapt grain, color, camera density, and text motion to the requested music rather than forcing cyber-grunge. For dark-pop/trap/cyber-grunge, rough print texture, film jitter, halftone, scan offsets, micro-shakes, and bass-reactive compression may be used with restraint.
