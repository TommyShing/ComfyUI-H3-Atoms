# Papercraft Stop-Motion Explainer

Explain the user's topic through a physically built miniature papercraft stage rather than a flat paper texture.

## Material and depth

- Build clear foreground, midground, background, and far-background planes with physical separation, occlusion, parallax, and real drop shadows.
- Make all visible objects from layered cardstock or cardboard: visible thickness, fibers, folds, seams, tabs, brads, joints, pull-tabs, sliders, rotating discs, cut edges, and matte tactile surfaces.
- Keep the main knowledge object readable in the middle plane. Use labels, arrows, cards, and symbols only when necessary and keep them from obscuring the mechanism.
- Avoid plastic 3D, glossy CG, flat vectors, live-action realism, glass, metallic sci-fi surfaces, cyberpunk neon, and paper-like texture pasted onto non-paper geometry.

## Explanation and motion

- Preserve domain-specific facts and turn the core idea into a physical paper mechanism, cross-section, journey, puppet demonstration, pop-up page, or miniature nature theater.
- Let each shot explain one knowledge beat through one dominant physical action.
- Use hand-manipulated stop-motion timing: stepped movement, tiny pauses, slight rebounds, hinged gestures, page flips, pull-tab reveals, sliding rails, rotating discs, pieces settling, and background mechanisms moving in parallax.
- Use miniature-camera language: static explanatory framing, slow push-in, lateral parallax, macro detail, slight top-down cross-section view, or controlled movement through layers. Avoid high-speed orbits, smooth CG transformation, liquid morphing, and impossible large puppet motion.
- Use paper-physics transitions such as page flip, pop-up unfold, pull-tab slide, paper-label wipe, cloud pass, circular paper mask, paper door, layer split, sticker reveal, or restrained confetti.

## Sound

- Synchronize paper flips, scissor snips, cardboard slides, rustles, clicks, pops, tape peels, joint taps, and box openings to visible actions.
- If narration exists, fit it inside the duration and keep music/SFX below it. Match music to the topic and culture rather than using a generic whimsical score.
