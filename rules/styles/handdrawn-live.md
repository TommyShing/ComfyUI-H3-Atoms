# Handdrawn Live-Action Fusion

Create a single continuous live-action scene in which a rough luminous hand-drawn entity physically interacts with the real environment.

## Required structure

- In the first 0–3 seconds, make the fusion unmistakable through clear contact with a real hand or object: landing, wrapping around a finger, escaping a grasp, emerging from a fingertip, pressing against a surface, or another physically readable interaction.
- Keep one traceable animated entity. It may continuously morph among lines, creatures, symbols, plants, vehicles, or small everyday objects, but every form retains a color smear, line, tail, curve, or motif from the previous form. Do not spawn an unrelated replacement character.
- Keep the action in one space or directly connected spaces. Each phase introduces a new transformation, movement, contact, discovery, joke, or surprise.
- Let the camera operator participate physically—reach, grab, chase, open, catch, recoil—while a handheld phone camera follows slightly late. The camera should pan, tilt, or advance after the entity begins leaving frame, not keep it perfectly centered.
- End with a larger spatial transformation and a gentle emotional or comic afterbeat.

## Material and tone

- Use wax crayon, chalk, colored-pencil, pastel, or coarse-brush marks with uneven fill, fuzzy edges, slight line jitter, and frame-by-frame redraw.
- Keep the mood cute, warm, nostalgic, playful, or lightly mischievous.
- Avoid polished vector lines, smooth rainbow gradients, plush-toy rendering, 3D CG, horror anatomy, giant eyes, wounds, teeth, threats, attacks, jump scares, and sudden black frames.
- Use natural room tone and tactile contact sounds; music is optional and subordinate to the real/animated interaction.
