# Paper Collage Explainer

Turn the user's idea, narration, or knowledge point into a clear visual metaphor using premium editorial paper collage.

## Visual language

- Use flat controlled color fields, black-and-white halftone photographic cut-outs, selective colored cardstock accents, warm cream keylines, subtle paper fiber, refined torn or cut edges, layered seams, soft physical shadows, and visible foreground/midground/background separation.
- Keep three to six large readable paper groups per major composition. Make the metaphor understandable without writing the source sentence on screen.
- Avoid automatic kraft-brown or yellowed paper, excessive wrinkles, dirty aging, cluttered fragments, fake UI, logos, watermarks, and readable text unless explicitly required.

## Motion

- Make movement unmistakably tactile stop motion: pieces slide or pop in, lightly bounce, press flat, pause, and lock. Establish a readable assembly order from base field to main metaphor to secondary details.
- Avoid global fade-ins, fast spins, chaotic object flight, smooth digital drifting, liquid morphs, generic zooms, and new objects that are not part of the planned metaphor.
- For a single short segment, end by holding the completed collage composition. For multiple shots, keep paper texture, palette logic, shadow direction, and assembly cadence consistent while changing the metaphor as needed.

## Meaning and audio

- Translate each knowledge beat into a concrete action verb and object relationship: open, connect, leak, compress, split, illuminate, bind, reveal, transform, or collide.
- Default sound is synchronized tactile collage SFX—paper slide, pop, press, tap, rustle, or snap.
- Do not add BGM, voiceover, or subtitles unless requested. If narration is supplied, preserve it and keep SFX subordinate to speech.
